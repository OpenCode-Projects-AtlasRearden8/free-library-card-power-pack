Museum & park passes

Museum passes and park passes in your glovebox — on the library's dime

Free days at museums and free entry to state parks, all from the circulation desk.

What it is

  • Many libraries lend real, physical passes to local museums, zoos, gardens, and science centers. For example, Berkeley Public Library's Discover & Go gets Berkeley cardholders free passes to Bay Area attractions — and the same program lends California State Park vehicle hangtags for up to two weeks. Hundreds of systems run similar programs under names like Museum Pass, Discover & Go, and Library Adventure Pass.
  • Check Out Colorado State Parks: every Colorado public library hands out a week-long state park pass and an adventure backpack (binoculars, field guides, activity cards) — an actual partnership between Colorado's libraries, State Library, and Parks & Wildlife.
  • Museums for All: not tied to your library card at all — if your family receives SNAP (food stamp) benefits, presenting an EBT card and photo ID at the door gets free or reduced admission at more than 1,600 participating museums nationwide.

Cost

  • Free or included with your library card for library pass programs (count of family members covered varies by museum).
  • Museums for All is free admission or reduced admission ($0–$3 at many museums) with an EBT card.
  • A few libraries lend passes on a first-come basis and ask a small refundable deposit — rare, but ask when you reserve.

What you need to sign up

  • Library pass programs: reserve through your library's website (search your library name plus 'museum pass'), by phone, or at a desk. Passes get checked out like a book, to your valid library card, often up to a week at a time.
  • Colorado: check out the pass-and-backpack from any participating Colorado public library for up to seven days (the pass covers one vehicle's entry).
  • Museums for All: nothing to sign up for — just show an EBT card and photo ID at a participating museum. Find them at the official Museums for All museum finder.

Good to know: Passes go fast on weekends and school holidays — reserve early or go mid-week. If your town has no museum-pass program, ask a librarian anyway: many systems teach out-of-town deals or can point you to the closest program that does.
